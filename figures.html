PRODUCTION NOTES

Making these thesis-ready

  • One palette everywhere: teal = emergence / RQ1, gold = normalisation / RQ2, terracotta = containment / RQ3. Use it in figures, slides and posters.
  • Tools: conceptual diagrams in Figma, PowerPoint or draw.io. The data figures (F2, F7, F13) can be regenerated from a CSV in R/ggplot2 or Python so they update as the corpus grows.
  • Captions that argue: start each caption with the claim (“News merges three different products…”), then explain the visual.
  • Accessibility: keep text labels on every coloured element so nothing depends on colour alone. Export SVG or PDF for the thesis, PNG at 300 dpi for Word.
TYPOGRAPHY NOTES

Academic best practice for figures

  • Sans-serif in figures, serif in text. APA 7 and most journal figure guidelines ask for a sans-serif font (Arial or Helvetica) inside figures. Reading text stays serif.
  • Size. No label smaller than 8 pt once the figure is placed in the thesis (and 8–14 pt across the figure).
  • Two typefaces at most across figures, posters and slides, and use them consistently.
  • Numbers in tables use aligned (tabular) digits, right-aligned in their column.
  • Captions. APA 7 puts the figure number and title above the figure and notes below. Many UK humanities styles caption below. Follow your department’s guide and be consistent.
  • Black and white first. Shading, hatching and labels carry meaning, never colour alone.
